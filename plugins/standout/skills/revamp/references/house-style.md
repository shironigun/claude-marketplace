# House style

A branded, enterprise profile: it reads like a strong resume written for a stranger, not a social post. Every claim is a CONFIRMED fact from the ledger. This style came out of a real profile revamp; the reasons are kept so you can judge new cases.

## Rules for every section

- **Facts from the ledger only.** Cite the fact IDs on the section's `Facts:` line. A number that is not in a cited fact does not go in. The check script enforces this.
- **Choose the words yourself.** The user supplies facts and approves; you write.
- **Plain words.** No "passionate", "results-driven", "dynamic", "seasoned", "guru", "ninja", "synergy", "leverage", "delve". No hashtags, no emoji except the two bullet glyphs below.
- **Typography.** No em dashes (use a comma, colon or full stop). Write "Resume" in plain letters, never with accents. No invisible characters.
- **Credit people.** Say who else did the work ("with a support analyst", "the engineering team built it"). Never claim a team's result as yours alone.
- **Own-measured numbers say so** ("by my tracking"). A number that looks audited when it is not is the fastest way to lose a reader's trust. Otherwise leave the source in the ledger: no "per the dashboard" or internal tool names in the profile text.
- **No title claims.** Experience titles match the employer's records. A headline may describe the work with a positioning label, but only one the user has approved, and never on the banner as if it were a title.
- **No years-of-experience count** unless the user asks for it. It dates the profile and invites filtering.
- **Selective, not complete.** Pick the strongest items for the target reader. A profile that lists everything says nothing.
- **Unicode bold only for headings and short labels** (𝗛𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴, or a two or three word label before a colon). Body text stays plain: LinkedIn search does not match bold letters and screen readers stumble on them. Use the sans-serif bold set (𝗔𝗕𝗖), the same one everywhere. Each bold character counts as 2 toward LinkedIn's limits.
- **Two bullet glyphs, each with one job:** ▪ for what the person did or built, ✔ for results and recognition.

## Limits

| Field | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 220 characters | The first words show in search and next to every comment. |
| About | 2,600 characters | Use most of it. Only the first two or three lines show before "see more". |
| Experience description | 2,000 characters per role | |
| Skill name | 80 characters | |

LinkedIn counts UTF-16 units, so bold letters count twice. Its own counter in each edit form wins if it ever disagrees with this table. Other fields (project and media descriptions, Featured) have no limit the check enforces; keep them short.

## Headline

`<Role identity> | <what they do, for whom, with which strength> | <the result or proof they are known for>`

- Role identity first: the official title, or an approved positioning label.
- The middle names the work in words the target reader searches for.
- The last part is one proof point: a result, a scale, a recognised credential.
- Use most of the 220 characters. Write three options; the user picks.

## About

The shape, top to bottom:

1. **Hook: two short lines.** They are all most readers see. Name the problem the person solves, then their answer to it. No greeting, no name, no third person.
2. **One value line**: what they do, in one sentence.
3. **One short paragraph**: where they work now and the scope (team size, kind of company, what they own). Use the public wording for anything NEEDS SCRUBBING.
4. **Bold sections**, a blank line between each, one point per line. Pick the four to seven that fit the person, for example: What I bring, What I've led, Tools I created, Results, Teaching, Recognition, What I believe.
   - "What I bring" uses ▪ with a bold two or three word label: `▪ 𝗟𝗮𝗯𝗲𝗹: one line of what that means in practice`.
   - Lists of work use ▪; results and recognition use ✔.
   - Lead bullets with the number when there is one.
5. **What I believe**: two to four short lines of the person's point of view. This is where they sound like a person.
6. **Close**: `Let's talk: <topic> · <topic> · <topic>`, then a link line if they have a portfolio or site.

Why this shape: a wall of bullets reads as a list of duties, and pure narrative hides the numbers. Short labelled sections let a stranger scan in ten seconds and still find the proof.

## Experience

One entry per role, current role first.

- **Title, company, dates, employment type** in the form fields, exactly as on the employer's records. Location only if the user wants it.
- **Scope line** (first line of the description): what the role owns, in one sentence, no "I".
- **▪ bullets**: start each with a strong past-tense verb, a different verb each time (Led, Cut, Designed, Specified, Wrote, Trained, Launched). Action, then result. Put the work that matches the target first.
- **Resume voice.** No "I'm", no added contractions, no self-praise. A humanizer may push for a casual voice here; keep the resume voice anyway.
- **Recognition and credentials under their own bold headings** inside the entry (𝗥𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗴𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻, 𝗖𝗲𝗿𝘁𝗶𝗳𝗶𝗲𝗱, 𝗧𝗮𝗹𝗸𝘀 & 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗽𝘀), one ✔ per item with month and year. Never squeeze several into one bullet.
- **Older or unrelated roles**: a scope line and one or two bullets.
- **Skills**: attach three to five skills to each role. **Media**: attach proof (a deck, a guide, a certificate) titled as the item itself.

## Featured

Three to five items, the strongest proof first.

- **Title the item as itself**, with the event and year when it helps: `Triage before you automate (Example Support Summit 2025)`, `Resume: <Name>, <Role>`, `Portfolio`. Never a pointer such as "My post on the talk". Replace the title LinkedIn pre-fills from a link.
- **Description**: one or two lines saying what it is and why open it.

## Projects

- **Name** as the item itself. **Dates**, and the role it belongs to.
- **Description**: one summary sentence, then `▪ Problem:` and what was wrong, then verb-first ▪ bullets for what the person did and what changed, then an ownership line when others or AI agents built parts: `Specified the work and reviewed every release; <who> built it.`

## Certifications

- **Form fields**: the exact name, issuer, issue date, credential ID and URL from the certificate.
- **Attach the certificate as media**, titled `<Name>: Certificate of <Completion, Achievement, ...>`, as the certificate itself says.
- **Media description**: one line of what it is (exam or course, hours, provider), three to five ▪ lines of what it covered, and one `Applied in ...` line tied to a CONFIRMED fact, if there is one. Call a course a course.

## Skills

- Skills the target roles search for, in LinkedIn's standard names (accept its autocomplete).
- The most important first; the user pins the top ones.
- Only skills a CONFIRMED fact supports. No programming languages or tools as a headline skill unless the target role asks for them.

## Banner brief

The plugin writes the brief; the user or a designer makes the image (Canva, Figma, or an HTML page at the canvas size, screenshotted).

- **Canvas**: 1584 x 396 px.
- **Always a gradient background**: soft radial glows over a diagonal linear gradient. Give every variant its own palette, with hex values.
- **Information-rich**: a value statement framed around the people the person helps, three proof points (CONFIRMED numbers), and a credibility band (certifications, awards, a talk). Sparse banners waste the space.
- **Two or three variants, each with different facts** (no number repeated across variants; the check warns), so the user can choose or rotate. Each variant is its own `## Banner brief: Variant X` section with the banner's words in the ```text block.
- **Readable**: nothing smaller than about 20 px at 1584 wide; LinkedIn shows the banner at roughly half size on a desktop.
- **Safe zones**: the profile photo covers the bottom-left. On phones the photo circle is much larger; on one phone measured in 2026, anything below about y = 215 had to start right of about x = 510. Ask the user to check the result on their own phone.
- **No title line** unless it is the official title.

## Draft file format

`draft.md` in the workspace, one `## Section` per paste target, in this order: Headline, About, Experience (one per role), Featured (one per item), Project (one per project), Certification (one per certificate), Skills, Banner brief (one per variant). Under each heading: a `Facts:` line, then one ```text block with exactly what gets pasted, then any notes for the user. See `examples/draft.md`.
