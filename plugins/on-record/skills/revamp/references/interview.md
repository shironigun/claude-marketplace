# The interview

The interview turns UNCONFIRMED facts into CONFIRMED ones and fills the gaps the sources left. Ask about facts. Choose the wording yourself: never ask the user to write a bullet, a headline or a summary.

## How to ask

- Rounds of up to five questions, numbered, so the user can answer "1: yes, 2: 14 people".
- Highest impact first: what the headline and About depend on, then the current role, then everything else.
- One fact per question. Say what you already have and where it came from: "A chat from March says the rollout cut handling time by 30%. Is that right, and who measured it?"
- Offer options when the answer is a choice: "Led it, built it with others, or helped?"
- Record each answer in the ledger at once: update the fact, set its status, add the interview as a source (with the date).
- Stop asking about a fact when the user does not know. It stays UNCONFIRMED and stays off the profile. Write a concrete scope fact instead of an outcome you cannot back.

## Round 1: target and rules (before any other question)

1. Who should read this profile, and what should they do after? (Recruiters for a named role, clients, peers, investors.)
2. What do you want to be known for, in your words? (You will rewrite it; this is the raw fact.)
3. Your official job titles, exactly as your employer records them. Do you use a different label for your work? (A headline may describe the work; an Experience title must match the employer's records.)
4. What is off limits? Employer or client names, product names, internal numbers, colleagues' names, location, years of experience, anything under NDA or company policy.
5. Anything coming up you do not want announced yet (a job search, a move, a launch)?

## Questions for every result

A result is the strongest kind of fact, so it needs the most care. Ask each of these until you have enough:

- **What changed?** Before and after, in the unit people use (hours, %, money, people, releases, customers).
- **How do you know?** Which report, dashboard, survey or document. If it is the user's own tracking, the profile says so ("by my tracking").
- **Over what period?** A number without a period cannot be checked.
- **What was your part?** Led, designed, specified, built, wrote, trained, presented, assisted, contributed to.
- **Who else did the work?** Name the team or role so the profile credits them. Ask whether a named colleague agrees to be named.
- **Can you show it?** A deck, an article, a certificate, a public link. Proof becomes Featured or media.
- **Is it public?** PUBLIC-SAFE, NEEDS SCRUBBING (then: what is the public wording?) or INTERNAL ONLY.

## Other gaps to close

- Dates for every role, project and certification (month and year).
- Status of anything in progress: done, in pilot, in review, planned. "In pilot" is a fact of its own; a plan is PLANNED and stays off the profile.
- Certifications versus courses: was there an exam or assessment?
- Contradictions between sources: show both versions and ask which is right.
- Recognition: who gave it, when, and what it was for.
- Proof for Featured: the three to five items they would most want a stranger to open.

## The approval gate

When the open questions that block the headline, About and current role are closed, show the user:

1. The counts: CONFIRMED, UNCONFIRMED, PLANNED, INTERNAL ONLY.
2. Every CONFIRMED fact you plan to use, grouped by section, with its source.
3. What stays out and why (UNCONFIRMED, PLANNED, INTERNAL ONLY, off limits).
4. Remaining open questions, if any.

Ask for an explicit OK. Then set the ledger's status line to `Status: approved <date>`. If facts change later, set it back to `Status: draft` and ask again.
